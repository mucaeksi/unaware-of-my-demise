# World Rules — The Law of Hours

Approved 2026-09-27; rules 6–10 added 2026-09-29 (world foundation W1, decisions C15–C21); rule 11 added 2026-09-29 (W2, C26). These rules constrain all future story, quest and event writing. Term definitions: `glossary.md`.

1. **An hour is a substance.** Every life holds a counted number of hours. Hours can be taken, given or held as debt ("Borrowed Hour", "HOUR STILL OWED", "one hour that belongs to you").
2. **Bells count; they do not heal.** The seven bells act as a ledger and ring to record each hour taken. They need no clapper: the debt makes the sound. Each ring accelerates Fadyme's curse.
3. **Names are witnesses.** An hour cannot be written without a name that remembers it. As the curse advances, the bearer's name is erased, because the ledger begins to count her as paid. Writing the name back (7-3) does not cancel the debt; it delays payment.
4. **The Unwritten Hour** is the one hour never recorded: the last hour before Crownfall, which Cael removed from time and keeps. Rewriting it requires enough paid hours. The term means only this hour; Fadyme's seal is the **Last Bell seal** (C17).
5. **Consent limit.** An hour can only be transferred with a witness and consent. This is why Cael must ask Mahmut for his hour rather than take it.

6. **The Ledger.** The seven bells are seven entries of one ledger. When a person dies, their hours are closed by the witness of the names that remember them, and the dead cross. A death with no witness cannot be closed. (C15)
7. **A bell is a place, not bronze.** A bell is its entry in the Ledger: a site, a name, a count. Bronze cracks and is recast; the count passes into the new metal. A shard carries its bell's count and trembles when that bell's debt moves. (C15)
8. **The guarantor is the bell line.** House Veyr bound the bells, so when the Ledger cannot collect a debt it collects from Veyr blood, but only from the **bell line**, which passes from mother to daughter. Sons are Veyr by name, not by bell. A bell-line daughter sealed to the Ledger is its **Last Bell**: the seven bells count, the Last Bell pays. (C16, C17)
9. **Distance slows, never stops.** The bells collect from a bearer at any distance. A bell ringing near her collects faster; the farther she lives from every bell, the slower she pays. (C15)
10. **Calling bells stay tuned.** An Inquisitor's calling bell stays tuned to the bearer it called and sounds when her debt moves, whether or not its clapper can swing. (C21)
11. **What leaves the Ledger leaves memory.** Whatever is taken out of the Ledger stops being witnessed. Events inside the Unwritten Hour are known to no one, and a person who steps out of recorded time fades from memory: people remember the office ("the chancellor who died at Crownfall") but cannot hold the name or the face. Only someone holding something the person wrote can still recognize them. (C26)

## Derived lore

- **Hollows** are bodies left by unpaid hours: dead whose names are erased but whose hours are not spent. Anything that erases names (erasure orders, stolen registers, blank name boards, a silent bell) makes Hollows. (rule 6)
- **The Offering:** when the kingdom cannot pay the Ledger (plague, war, a silent bell), the Crown seals a bell-line daughter so her hours pay the debt. The first Offering made the First Eclipse. (C17)
- **Recast bells keep counting:** the Flint forge recast the Harvest Bell "for festivals"; the count moved into their bronze. Shards count too: the Ash-Maw's jaw-bell (Whispering Bell), Pippa's charm (Silent Bell). (rule 7)
- **Why Fadyme lasted six years:** the seven bearers before her were held at the Altar as living anchors and were spent in about ten years together; Fadyme lived far from every bell. The Part I road passes every bell, which is why it is fatal. (rule 9, C18)
- **How Yseldra finds her:** her tied calling bell still sounds whenever Fadyme's debt moves. (rule 10, C21)
- **Where time already stopped, it can be turned:** the First Eclipse is a moment the Ledger has held open for generations. Cael's Hinge (the Eclipse Construct) uses it to make the Altar count backward. Player-facing only from Part II. (C20)
- **Why no one names C.V.:** Mahmut served three years at court and Mirel was a palace scribe, yet neither can place the initials: Cael's name has faded with his step out of the Ledger (rule 11). Fadyme recognizes the hand because she carries her father's last page, which Cael wrote. She has never said the name aloud. (C26)
- **Who killed the king** happened inside the Unwritten Hour, so no living person knows; the answer returns only if the hour is rewritten. (C26)
- **Witnessing buys time:** keeping the bearer's name witnessed delays her payment (rule 3). Mirel's rewrite at 7-3 is why Part I ends with "weeks" left despite the pace of the road; Iona reading the name every night and Clem writing it every day keep delaying it. It never cancels the debt. (C29)
